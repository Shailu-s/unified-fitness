"To eat" not "left"

At zero, 120g protein left would be wrong — nothing has been spent, so nothing is "left". The label flips to Protein to eat today. The number is the same; the word matches the situation.

Zero bars still show their track

Empty bars keep full height with 0% sitting on the track in ink, since no fill exists to hold a white label. An empty track reads as a thing waiting to be filled — which is the message.

Burned is still real

412 burned stays, because it comes from the phone's sensors, not from logging. Even on a blank day the app already knows something about you — proof it works before you have done anything.

"Usuals" solve the cold start

Two one-tap shortcuts for this user's most-logged foods. The first entry of the day costs a single tap instead of a photo, a wait and a correction. Manual-entry friction is the top documented reason people abandon fitness apps — this is the cheapest place to attack it.

No illustration, no apology

"Nothing yet today." states the fact and moves on. No cartoon plate, no "Oops, your diary is empty!" — an empty screen is an invitation to act, and the fastest way to act is the shutter already sitting below.

Where the "usuals" come from

Day one there is no history, so this list starts as common Indian breakfasts and fills in with real habits after a few days. By week two it should be the two things this person actually eats every morning — which is when the app starts feeling like it knows them.
