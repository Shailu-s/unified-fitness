Protein leads, not calories

52g protein left is the hero. Calories are easy to hit and easy to guess; protein is the number people actually miss — especially on a vegetarian-heavy Indian diet. Leading with it makes the app opinionated about what matters.

Three bars, one block

Protein, fibre and calories sit together as a single instrument rather than three scattered widgets. Bars are 26px — thick enough to carry the percentage inside the fill, right-aligned at the leading edge so the number travels with progress.

Tight top, deep gap, then the log

Hero and bars are pulled together into one protein block — the number and its bar belong to each other. Then a fixed 66px gap separates the day's targets from the day's record. Silence does the dividing, so no rule or card is needed.

The shutter

A real 68px camera shutter, centred, with type and voice as 46px satellites. The shape is borrowed from a camera app, so the gesture is understood before a word is read. "Snap your plate" is the only copy needed.

"Eaten so far" for the table

Not "Logged today" — that is system vocabulary, describing what the database did. Eaten so far is the user's own words for the same thing, and "so far" quietly implies the day is not over.

The risk worth naming

This design stakes the main interaction on photo accuracy — and the Sydney study found AI apps off by up to 49% over and 76% under on mixed dishes, which is most Indian food. The shutter must therefore open an editable draft, never commit a number silently. Type and voice stay one tap away for anyone who wants precision.

Burned is context, not a target

Goal stays 2,100 — untouched. Burned is one quiet line under the bar with a Move-pink dot: 412 burned today. No second bar, no marker, no raised goal. It informs without asking to be decoded, because burn is not something you are trying to fill.

Over 100%

The fill caps at full width and the label reads 150% in place. For calories that is an overshoot; for protein it is a win. The metric decides whether over is good or bad.

Photo is primary, not exclusive

Camera gets the filled dark button; voice and type sit beside it. Research says photo estimates are unreliable on mixed Indian food, so a photo must land in an editable draft, never silently commit a number. Three ways in, one obvious default.

Indian portions in the log

Entries read "1 katori", "2 roti", "1 plate" — not grams. HealthifyMe already does this and users expect it. Grams-only input is a non-starter here.

What I left out

No carbs/fat bars, no meal-type grouping (breakfast/lunch/dinner), no water tracker, no streak badge. All are standard in competitors and all were cut. Say the word if any earns its place.
